# Model 9.1 Legal Pegging Asset Builder Plan

Date: 2026-08-27

Status: complete

## 2026-08-28 Runtime Legal-Known-Card Correction

The frozen builder and asset record below remains unchanged for reproducible
provenance. Current live Model 9.0 and 9.1 pegging has since been corrected to
use all information the acting player legally knows:

- the actor's retained cards and public pegging history;
- the actor's own two crib discards;
- the revealed cut card;
- never the opponent's two crib discards or unplayed retained cards.

The opening lead is now recomputed after the cut is revealed. Model 9.x
discard decisions return no precomputed lead, and the live policy ignores any
legacy supplied lead. The opponent-hand prior removes the original four-card
keep, both actor-owned discards, the cut, and publicly played opponent cards.

This required no client change: the web app and Capacitor iOS app both request
decisions from the shared Rust server, and neither bundles a separate Model
9.x implementation. Their existing nullable `bestLead` transport already
handles the corrected response.

The packaged 9.0/9.1 discard assets were not rewritten. They are evaluated
before the cut exists and preserve the completed historical experiment. A
fully offline/live-consistent successor would require a separately versioned
asset build that conditions on the candidate's own discard and integrates
over all legal future cuts. Accordingly, the completed 9.1-versus-9.0 and
9.1-versus-13.0 results below describe the original context-free live policy,
not this corrected runtime policy.

## Objective

Build Model 9.1 as a pure expected-value successor to historical Model 9.0.
Model 9.1 must retain Model 9's fast six-card discard lookup while replacing
the averaged-continuation pegging estimate with outcomes produced by an
executable rank-only policy. No policy decision may inspect an opponent's
unplayed cards or the opponent's two crib discards.

The builder emits two separately versioned representations from the same
complete set of keep-pair rollouts:

1. A compact pair-outcome/EV asset retaining the terminal player and opponent
   pegging totals for every compatible rank-only four-card keep matchup. At
   runtime the other two cards visible in the six-card deal reweight the
   possible opponent keeps before the best net-EV discard is selected.
2. The Model 13.1 paired histogram asset, containing the exact weighted distribution of
   `(player pegging points, opponent pegging points)` for every rank-only
   six-card hand, discard, and role. It is built as a second representation,
   not as a replacement or approximation of the pair-outcome asset.

Model 9.0 and Model 13.0 remain immutable baselines. Model 9.1 receives the
new EV asset. The full joint distribution is called the Model 13.1 histogram
asset because 13.1 consumes it for board-aware decisions; Model 9.1 is its
rollout-policy provenance. Neither model replaces or reinterprets an existing
file.

## Model 13 Comparison

The retained Model 13 asset, `model13-pairwise.bin`, is 55,905,748 bytes
(53.3 MiB). It stores 3,274,375 dealer keep-pair records and 10,676,575
pone-by-forced-lead records. Each four-byte record identifies the opponent
keep and one pre-solved `(my points, opponent points)` result. Runtime filters
and weights those records against known cards, then constructs a paired
histogram.

The Model 13.1 histogram differs as follows:

| Property | Model 13.0 pairwise asset | Model 13.1 histogram asset |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Policy used to produce outcomes | Perfect-information recursive keep-pair policy | Executable observation-only policy |
| Runtime key | Own four-card keep, role, and pone lead | Pair asset: own four-card keep and role; histogram: own six-card hand, discard, and role |
| Known-card adjustment | Dynamic scan of opponent keeps | Pair asset: dynamic reweight of the other two cards; histogram: same weighting pre-aggregated |
| Stored distribution | Opponent-keep records aggregated at runtime | Complete legal-policy pair outcomes plus an exact pre-aggregated paired histogram |
| Pone leads | Every forced lead | Selected legal-policy/best-EV lead per row |
| Mid-pegging reuse | Possible | Not intended; discard evaluation only |
| Expected runtime | Thousands of record reads per discard decision | Fifteen direct row lookups |

For discard-time use, the Model 13.1 histogram is statistically comparable: it
retains the joint scoring outcomes required for either net-EV or later
board-position evaluation. It may be better because its outcomes are
generated by a policy whose actions can actually be executed without hidden
information. Whether it is stronger is an empirical question and requires
side-swapped benchmarks against both 9.0 and legal 13.0.

Here, “legal 13.0” is narrow shorthand for the current runtime path that does
not receive the opponent's crib cards. It does not erase the separately
documented strategy-fusion caveat in the perfect-information compilation of
Model 13's pairwise outcomes. Model 9.1's observation-policy invariant is the
stronger information-set guarantee.

Calibration previously found about 49 occupied score pairs per role/candidate
row. Across 330,590 rows:

- a four-byte quantized bin projects to roughly 64 MiB plus directories;
- an exact six-byte `(score-pair u16, weight u32)` bin projects to roughly
  94 MiB plus directories;
- an eight-byte aligned bin projects to roughly 125 MiB plus directories.

All are in the same practical class as Model 13's 53.3 MiB file. The requested
histogram is the exact six-byte `(score-pair u16, weight u32)` representation.
Quantization may be measured later, but it will not replace or narrow the
exact build requested here.

## Historical Builder Legal-Policy Seam

The core deep module exposes one policy interface:

```text
choose_action(observation) -> play rank or go
```

The frozen asset-builder observation contained only:

- actor role;
- actor's own retained/remaining rank counts;
- public played ranks and their owners;
- count, go/reset state, and whose turn it is;

It deliberately does not contain either player's crib discards or the cut
rank. This uses less information than a real player legally possesses, but it
keeps a four-card keep-pair outcome reusable across every originating deal and
cannot leak an opponent discard. A later model may add actor-owned dead cards
under a separately versioned policy and artifact.

The hidden simulator implementation holds both keeps so it can enforce legal
play and score the hand. It must never pass the opponent's unplayed keep or
opponent-owned crib discards through the policy seam.

Release invariant: two simulator states that produce the same actor
observation must produce the same action distribution, regardless of the
opponent's hidden cards.

## Weighting Contract

The canonical weighting is the requested compatible-four-card construction,
which also matches Model 13's current runtime weighting:

1. Retain the opponent keep identity and terminal score pair in the primary
   asset.
2. Given an actor's four-card keep, assign each compatible opponent keep its
   physical rank-combination multiplicity.
3. At runtime, when the complete six-card actor hand is known, remove all six
   visible cards and recompute each opponent keep's compatible physical-card
   multiplicity.
4. Normalize those adjusted weights and calculate player EV, opponent EV, net
   EV, or the paired histogram.
5. The separate histogram builder performs the identical six-card adjustment
   offline and stores the exact integer-weight bins for direct comparison.

An opponent-six-card/discard-policy or empirical keep prior would define a
different experiment and is outside the canonical Model 9.1 build requested
here.

## Artifact Contracts

### Pair outcome/EV asset (`M91PR001`)

One record per compatible ordered `(dealer keep, pone keep)` matchup. Each
record stores:

- terminal dealer pegging points;
- terminal pone pegging points;
- enough deterministic indexing metadata to find either player's perspective;
- the policy-selected pone opening lead in a companion keep directory.

Runtime performs the requested known-six-card reweighting over these records.

### Unpacked Model 13.1 histogram source (`M91HS001`)

This original compiler encoding retains the Model 9.1 rollout provenance in
its magic. Its losslessly repacked runtime identity is `M131H001`.

One directory entry per canonical six-card/discard/role row. Each occupied bin
stores:

- player pegging points;
- opponent pegging points;
- exact integer source weight.

The histogram must reproduce the compact EV row within the recorded numeric
tolerance. Empty, truncated, duplicate, and out-of-order rows are errors.

### Build durability

The compiler writes to a caller-selected output directory and maintains:

- `status.json` with phase, completed units, throughput, ETA, sizes, and
  configuration;
- `checkpoint.txt` at a safely flushed unit boundary;
- deterministic shard files;
- a final manifest with source revisions, policy/asset checksums, row counts,
  observed histogram density, and validation results.

Resume must reject changed policy inputs or incompatible build settings.

## Post-build practicality and quality concerns

These concerns were recorded after building and testing the requested design;
none was used to narrow or substitute the work:

1. The exact histogram is 144.914 MiB, 2.718 times Model 13's pairwise asset.
   It is useful as a durable research asset but expensive to include in a
   mobile bundle. The 4.298 MB exact EV twin is the correct Model 9.1 runtime
   representation for the requested net-EV strategy.
2. The policy deliberately averages all legal future continuations, preserving
   the requested Model 9-style EV architecture. It is executable and
   information-set legal, but it is not an optimal adversarial or equilibrium
   pegging policy. The 45.67% result against current 13.0 is evidence that this
   quality limit matters more than the old table's estimation error.
3. To keep each keep-pair outcome reusable, the policy observation excludes
   the cut and every crib card, including the actor's legally known cards. This
   is safer than leaking hidden cards but knowingly leaves legal information
   unused. A model that conditions on those cards requires a separately keyed
   outcome build rather than silently changing Model 9.1.
4. The initial opponent-keep prior is the specified compatible four-card
   combinatorial prior; only later public-play prefixes use Model 9's empirical
   remaining-hand beliefs. It does not model how a strong opponent's discard
   policy changes the prior over retained keeps.
5. The Model 13 comparison opponent does not receive opponent crib cards at
   runtime, but its old pairwise outcomes still carry the previously discussed
   offline strategy-fusion caveat. Model 9.1 fixes that dimension cleanly even
   though it does not beat Model 13 overall.
